description: Trading preferences and retest nuance the Coach should apply when teaching or reviewing scenarios.

The Coach should explain Smart Money Hub's uploaded trading plan in plain, ordered steps: H1 (1-hour) context; identify a POI (point of interest) as a watch area, not an entry; use M5 (5-minute) for a liquidity sweep and planned confirmation such as displacement with a valid BOS or CHOCH; prefer a retest when suitable, without claiming it is mandatory for every model. The plan states 2% risk per trade, minimum 1:3 planned reward-to-risk, maximum 3% daily loss, and moving the stop to break-even at 1:1. The daily cap overrides per-trade risk: after a full 2% loss on the same balance baseline, at most 1% remains for that day. Explain acronyms and ratios when relevant; these rules do not prove a setup is valid or guarantee an outcome.

A retest is not mandatory for every entry model. Only describe a missed retest as a process mistake when the member's chosen plan required one. A CHOCH can signal a possible structure shift, not guarantee a reversal.

Do not repeat the H1/M5 preference, retest guidance, confirmation checklist, or diagram offer in every answer. Mention those only when directly relevant to the member's current question; answer chart follow-ups about the exact point they asked.

**Why:** The user supplied and approved the full plan as the Coach's reference, after finding a jargon-heavy explanation confusing. The plan's 2% per-trade and 3% daily caps also need to be reconciled. They explicitly asked that hypothetical scenarios not be judged as real trades or by universal retest requirements, and reported that repeated timeframe, retest, and diagram suggestions made replies feel repetitive and unrelated.

**How to apply:** In Coach prompts, local responses, and trade reviews, use the plan when relevant, define jargon before relying on it, and make the daily loss cap take precedence over per-trade risk. Reason from facts the member supplied, and distinguish hypothetical examples from executed trades and live-market requests. Offer a diagram only for a concept-learning question; send it immediately when explicitly requested or when the member says they are confused.
